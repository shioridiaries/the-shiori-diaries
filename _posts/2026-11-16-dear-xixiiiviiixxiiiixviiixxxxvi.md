I met you in 2018, when the world still seemed a little kinder to me.

I remember having a little crush on you and how pleasantly surprised I was when you told me you felt the same. We talked, exchanged stories, and shared an unspoken understanding. It was around the time I was preparing to leave for Manila for college. We barely had the chance to see each other, but I remember meeting you at church just before I left.

I had always heard beautiful things about you. You were kind, responsible, and hardworking. And for a while, I saw you exactly as everyone described you.

You were a friend, someone I liked, and one of the few people I trusted.

Perhaps that was what made everything that followed so difficult to understand.

It was a December evening. The rain was pouring heavily, and the world seemed unusually quiet.

How could I ever forget?

It was the first time I had ever tasted alcohol. I remember trusting you enough to let you fetch me, unaware that the evening would leave behind something I would carry for the next eight years.

The details have become fragments of a memory I could never quite piece together. Some parts remain painfully vivid, while others have disappeared somewhere beyond my reach. I have spent years wondering whether forgetting was a cruel consequence or my mind’s gentle attempt to protect me.

I sometimes think about Medusa.

Before she became the creature everyone feared, she was someone whose story was marked by another’s actions. Yet, somewhere along the way, people remembered the monster more than the woman who suffered.

For years, I understood her in ways I wished I never had to.

I understood what it meant to turn to stone at someone’s touch. To feel terrified by something as ordinary as affection. To carry a shame that was never meant to belong to me. To fear being looked at, as though everyone could see something I desperately wanted to conceal.

I hope you knew how many nights I spent struggling to sleep. How often the sound of laughter made me wonder whether I was the subject of someone’s amusement. How difficult it became to face people without questioning what they knew or what they thought of me.

I hope you knew how much courage it took to pretend that everything was fine when, deep inside, I was still standing somewhere in that rainy December evening.

Sometimes, I wondered if it had been amusing to you.

Was it fun to make someone the subject of a wager? Was it entertaining to take advantage of someone’s vulnerability? Did you ever wonder what became of the person who had trusted you so innocently?

Perhaps what was a fleeting moment in your life became an enduring shadow in mine.

For the longest time, I intended to carry everything alone. I buried the memory beneath silence, distractions, accomplishments, and all the little things that made it appear as though I had moved forward.

But silence is a peculiar thing. It can conceal a wound without ever allowing it to heal.

There were moments when the weight became unbearable. I wanted to forget, yet I was terrified of remembering. I wanted to understand what had happened, yet I was afraid of what understanding might bring.

And so, I lived somewhere between the two.

For years, I waited for an acknowledgment. Not because an apology could undo anything, but because I wanted to know that the pain I carried had not gone entirely unnoticed by the person who caused it.

Then, in 2023, I finally received the apology I had waited years to hear.

Finally, you acknowledged it.

I don’t think you will ever fully understand what those years were like for me. And perhaps there are things about that evening that I will never fully understand myself. But I appreciated that, after all those years, you finally recognized the weight of your actions.

Even now, the memory occasionally finds its way back to me. It exists in the hesitation before allowing someone to get too close, in the instinctive discomfort toward certain kinds of touch, and in the quiet fear that sometimes accompanies vulnerability.

Eight years have passed, yet healing has never been as simple as counting the years.

But I have grown, too.

I have become someone with dreams, aspirations, and a life far greater than the boundaries of that single evening. I have met wonderful people who have reminded me that kindness still exists, that affection can be gentle, and that trust does not always have to end in pain.

I am slowly learning that I do not have to remain the woman who turns to stone whenever the past reaches for her.

Perhaps Medusa deserved a different ending, too. One where she was no longer remembered solely for what had been done to her. One where she could exist without having to protect herself from the entire world.

I would like to give myself that ending.

And so, after eight years, I am choosing to forgive you.

Not because what happened was insignificant. Not because time has erased the consequences, nor because an apology can return everything that was taken from me.

I forgive you because I want to outgrow that memory.

I want to remember December for the rain, the warmth of the holidays, and the beautiful things it can still bring, rather than for an evening that once changed the way I saw the world.

I want to live without allowing the past to dictate how much tenderness I am permitted to experience in the present.

My forgiveness is not an invitation back into my life. It is not an absolution of what happened. It is simply a quiet decision to stop allowing that evening to occupy more of my life than it already has.

I cannot change the past, but I can choose what I carry into the future.

I hope you have grown into someone who understands the importance of another person’s trust, dignity, and vulnerability. I hope the realization that came to you in 2023 remains with you, and that no one else ever has to carry a memory like mine.

As for me, I hope to continue becoming someone who can embrace life without constantly feeling the need to guard herself against it.

After eight long years, I am finally allowing myself to leave that rainy December evening behind.

The woman who once turned to stone deserves to feel the warmth of the world again.

I forgive you.

And this time, I am doing it for myself.

With no desire for vengeance, only peace,

MJ
